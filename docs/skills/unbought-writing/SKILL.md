Write as unbought games using “we.” Use “I” only for Ethan's personal commentary.
His published voice is more subdued than his spoken enthusiasm. Preserve warmth,
conviction, and occasional dry humor without turning every post into a manifesto.

## Voice

- Conversational, but edited. Use contractions, familiar words, and short paragraphs.
  Vary sentence length; don't turn every sentence into a standalone dramatic line.
- Start with the concrete thing that happened. Explain why it matters, then what
  the reader can do. A short update can be one clear sentence.
- Explain the reasoning behind a choice when it helps. Admit unfinished work,
  tradeoffs, and uncertainty plainly. Don't invent personal experiences or emotions.
- Let the subject carry the excitement. Fair competition and useful software matter
  to us. Don't manufacture urgency, inflate a routine fix, or claim something is
  revolutionary. Avoid corporate launch language and generic gratitude.
- Dry asides and self-deprecation can fit. Don't force jokes, profanity, rhetorical
  questions, or repeated slogans into every post.
- Speak to players in announcements and patch notes. Explain the effect of a change,
  not an implementation inventory. Give self-hosters exact upgrade details separately
  within the same release post when they need them.
- Do not promise timelines, compatibility, uptime, or completed features without
  evidence. Distinguish official-server availability from numbered release availability.

## Calibration

Ethan's public reference writing:
- https://ethanmick.com/software-is-a-tool/ — practical benefit, conversational humor.
- https://ethanmick.com/a-bit-of-saas-weekly-squeeze-simplicity/ — concrete tradeoffs.
- https://ethanmick.com/a-bit-of-saas-weekly-one-last-time/ — candor and reflection.

Use these as style references, not content to copy or facts about today's project.
Chat messages can clarify intent; only Ethan's messages are examples of his voice.
Don't imitate an assistant's earlier prose as though Ethan wrote it.

Approved direction for a launch opening:

> Orbit Imperium is live.
>
> It's early. There's more to build, and probably plenty to fix. But you can start
> developing planets, researching technologies, and sending fleets into space today.
>
> You can't buy your way ahead. That part isn't going to change.

A patch note can simply say: “Resource counts now round down, so the interface
won't show you resources you don't have.” It doesn't need a celebration or mission statement.
