Orbit Imperium 0.7.0 gives moons a working place in your empire. Planetary battles
can create them, Lunar Bases make room to build, Sensor Phalanxes reveal fleet
movements around nearby planets, and Jump Gates move ships between your moons.

There's more to do between flights, too. Link your research labs, give your
alliance a home, and see your fleets in one place. We've also worked
through player reports about dispatch, combat planning, notifications, and the
small things that get in the way when you're playing every day.

These notes describe the numbered open-source release. Availability on an existing
server depends on its deployment, content activation, and optional integrations.

## Build and operate from moons

Battles at planets can now create a moon for the defender. Each 100,000 metal and
crystal in newly generated debris adds one percentage point to the chance, up to
20%. Existing debris and deuterium don't increase that chance. A planet can have
one moon, with its own ships, resources, construction, and fleet activity.

Lunar Bases add room for facilities. Build Robotics Factories and Shipyards,
manufacture ships and defenses, and use moons for fleet missions. Moons don't
produce resources or conduct research; you'll need to supply them.

A **Sensor Phalanx** scans planets within its range for 5,000 deuterium from the
source moon. Each scan creates a private, historical report of visible fleet
movements. Opening that report again is free; a fresh observation costs another
scan. Moons cannot be scanned. Range grows with the completed Phalanx level.

**Jump Gates** instantly transfer stationed mobile ships between two owned moons
with completed gates. Resources stay behind, and jumping uses neither fuel nor
a fleet slot. Both gates cool down for one hour at universe speed 1, scaled by
universe speed. Higher gate levels don't shorten that cooldown. Construction
requires Lunar Base 1 and Hyperspace Technology 7.

Moon destruction and standalone moon abandonment remain unavailable. Removing a
parent planet also removes its moon. See the
[Phalanx guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/operations/sensor-phalanx.md)
and [Jump Gate guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/operations/jump-gates.md)
for operating and activation details.

## Link your laboratories

Each completed **Intergalactic Research Network** level brings one more eligible
planetary lab into a research project. The origin still pays the full cost, and
every contributing lab must meet the technology's lab requirement. You still have
one research slot.

Research details show the participating planets and lab levels. Once a project
starts, its contributions and completion time stay fixed. Research blocks lab
construction, and contributing colonies cannot be abandoned while it runs.

## A home for your empire and alliance

Empire profiles now have names, taglines, chosen titles, and lasting player
history. The layered insignia composer combines a center, accent, background,
and colors. Thanks to **Brian Mick ([@bmick](https://github.com/bmick))**, our first
external code contributor, for its foundation and original implementation in
[#302](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/pull/302).

Alliances have a Hall with a crest, artwork, motto, welcome message, and
noticeboard. Customization and image uploads are free, including animated GIFs
and WebPs. Leaders can delegate editing through roles. Recruitment pages offer
three layouts, Markdown stories, previews, and shareable invitations. Applications
still require approval where the alliance requires it.

Discovery, member lists, applications, settings, and activity history have clearer
homes and direct links. Audit entries use readable names and preserve new event
details when the original role or notice later changes.

Optional account integration adds Renown, permanent cosmetic rewards, automatic
earned titles, and invitations into a specific universe. These features depend on
the server's account service and rewards program. The community release includes
the integration and content formats; official paid and supporter-only collections
are supplied separately. Cosmetics and rewards don't grant resources, faster
fleets, or combat advantages.

## Better fleet planning and reports

- **A clearer fleet overview.** See the ships stationed at each world or switch
  to empire-wide totals, including ships in flight. Queued ships stay separate.
  Empire traffic combines your missions and detected incoming fleets in arrival
  order, with filters for yours and others.
- **Dispatch shows potential loot and available cargo room.** Simulator plans can
  open directly in final review after current checks, with launch still requiring
  confirmation. Cargo fill controls work while fleet slots are occupied, and
  deployments show their estimated arrival time.
- **Combat planning is easier to inspect.** Import an owned planet or moon as the
  defender, estimate hypothetical flights, and review surviving cargo, loot,
  generated debris, average rounds, moon chance, and recycler estimates. Unknown
  inputs default to zero and remain editable. Defense comparison also shows costs
  and combat values side by side, with optional completed-research bonuses.
  Estimates use your entered forces
  and universe rules; the simulator retains its 2,000 combined-unit limit.
- **Galaxy stays steady while traffic changes.** Compact rows keep their height,
  arrow keys move between systems, and coordinates open Galaxy with additional
  actions on right-click or long-press. Save a default probe count per universe
  or use **Spy with…** to choose a different fleet.
- **Reports are easier to work through.** Paginate and filter history, mark all
  reports read, reuse archive age settings, and archive with a keyboard shortcut.
  Displayed reports retain their read state. Combat reports show defender loot
  losses and separate ships from defenses; fresh espionage reports reveal stored
  missiles when the defense section is visible.

Notifications stack behind the newest card, order notices can be dismissed, and
long announcements scroll within their popup. Settings has focused pages, the
mobile menu is a full page, and brief connection interruptions recover more
quietly while preserving the current screen and edits. Session refresh and
cancelled-request cleanup fixes reduce unnecessary sign-outs and failed requests.

## Starting, taking a break, and leaving

New empires begin with an optional arrival scene and guided opening chapters.
The Briefing walks through ordinary construction, research, manufacturing, and
raids on personal outposts. Your ships, travel times, and combat rules are real;
there are no gifted fleets or speed multipliers. Existing empires retain the full
interface and can complete new directives through ordinary play.

**Vacation mode** requires clearing your fleets, missiles, queues, and visiting
holds before entry. It lasts at least 48 real-time hours and stops ordinary
fleet and economic actions while producing only base income. Saved production
settings resume when you return. Previously launched inbound missions still
resolve, so vacation doesn't cancel an attack already on its way.

You can request deletion of one empire with a seven-day cancellation period.
This leaves your account and other universes intact. Inactive empires receive
a cleanup warning after 28 days and are removed after 35 days; qualifying
foreground activity before removal resets that clock. Vacation exempts an empire
from inactivity cleanup, but doesn't cancel a voluntary deletion request.

## Upgrading from 0.6.0

Back up PostgreSQL and persistent asset storage, and retain the previous image.
Use the 0.7.0 binary and your database configuration for `orbit db-init`. The new
migrations are **0030–0032 and 0034–0047**; 0033 is intentionally absent. They add
empire lifecycle state, moons, Phalanx indexes, recruitment and identity records,
report filters, onboarding, preferences, rewards, invitations, analytics, and
gate-use records.

**Coordinate the API and worker upgrade.** Older workers cannot interpret the
new deletion events or vacation state. Deploy matching API, worker, operator,
and web builds before activating content. Migration 0031 converts existing
operator vacation flags into intervals with a new 48-hour minimum while retaining
pre-upgrade production. Do not run 0.6.0 writers against the upgraded state.

**Deployment enables inactivity cleanup immediately.** Workers adopt existing
memberships, and empires already inactive for 35 days become eligible for removal
at the next safe ordered instant. Review retained empires before starting the
upgraded workers. See [empire deletion](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/features/empire-deletion.md)
and [vacation compatibility](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/features/vacation.md).

The standard content bundle is **0.16.0**, independently versioned from the
application. Fresh universes receive it; existing universes keep their saved rules
until you explicitly upgrade with the matching binary:

```sh
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id> --dry-run
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id>
orbit rules export --universe <id> --out rules-after.json
orbit audit --universe <id>
```

Review conflicts with customized definitions and record the applied revision and
audit result. Already-paid work keeps its accepted terms. If activation is pending,
keep workers running and repeat the same command until confirmed. Phalanx activation
can reject an active returning deployment whose return cause is unknown; let it
settle, then retry. No historical backfill or fleet drain is required. Migration
0034 builds indexes under the existing schema preparation bounds; large or busy
databases may require diagnosis if preparation fails.

Alliance artwork uses persistent asset storage, which must be available to all
serving instances and included in backups. Operators can now publish and maintain
Orb announcements through `orbit announcements publish` without rebuilding the
game; each server owns its feed. See the
[alliance customization](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/operations/alliance-customization.md)
and [announcements](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/operations/announcements.md)
guides.

Standalone servers remain independent of Unbought Accounts. Optional rewards
require compatible account services, separately configured service credentials,
and installed content. Deploy compatible readers before activating version-3
cosmetic catalogs; older binaries cannot read newly saved version-3 recipes.
Legacy invitations without a universe binding need fresh links.
`ORBIT_ACCOUNT_URL` optionally enables the account-menu link. Optional analytics
now records acquisition and enrollment on the server. Follow the
[community rewards guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/operations/community-rewards.md)
and [rules upgrade guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.7.0/docs/operations/rules-updates.md).

Prefer a forward fix after new state has been written. Turning off a feature does
not make retained history readable by an older binary. Recovery to an incompatible
release requires its matching pre-upgrade backup, accounting for activity since
that backup.
