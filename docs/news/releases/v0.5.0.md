Orbit Imperium 0.5.0 adds private messages, friends, and alliance circulars. You
can talk to another player from the galaxy, rankings, or a report, and send an
announcement to your alliance without leaving the game.

Your empire can get stronger too: Bombers, Plasma Turrets, Large Shield Domes,
and Fusion Reactors. We've also shortened the trip from spotting a debris field
to sending recyclers, and from simulating a battle to preparing an attack.

These notes describe the numbered open-source release. Availability on an existing
server depends on its deployment and content activation. Self-hosters upgrading
from 0.4.0 need the coordinated upgrade below.

## Messages, friends, and alliance circulars

Private conversations keep their history, including when you hide a conversation
from your inbox. New messages bring it back. Friend requests require acceptance,
and either player can end the friendship. Blocking a player stops new messages,
friend requests, and circular delivery between you; it doesn't prevent attacks or
change game reports.

Alliance circulars send a message to the members present when it is sent. Leaders
can grant the sending permission through alliance roles. Existing alliances keep
their permissions, so leaders need to grant it to other roles explicitly.

Messages support basic Markdown. Attachments, editing, unsend, read receipts, and
player reporting tools aren't available yet. Polling keeps the conversation up to
date without pulling you away from older messages or interrupting composition.
See the [messages and friends guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.5.0/docs/operations/messages-and-friends.md).

## More choices for your planets and fleets

- **Bombers** specialize in attacking lighter defenses. They require Shipyard 8,
  Impulse Drive 6, and Plasma Technology 5. They use Impulse propulsion throughout;
  the later Hyperspace Drive upgrade isn't implemented.
- **Plasma Technology** requires Research Lab 4, Energy Technology 8, Laser
  Technology 10, and Ion Technology 5. It unlocks Bombers and Plasma Turrets, with
  no per-level mining or combat bonus.
- **Plasma Turrets** require Shipyard 8 and Plasma Technology 7. They have no
  incoming or outgoing rapid fire in the selected rules.
- **Large Shield Domes** require Shipyard 6 and Shielding Technology 6. Each planet
  can have one, alongside its Small Shield Dome. A dome's shield protects the dome
  itself, not the other units on the planet.
- **Fusion Reactors** turn deuterium into energy. They require Deuterium
  Synthesizer 5 and Energy Technology 3. Resource settings show actual fuel use,
  net deuterium production, and when fuel limits output. At zero stored deuterium,
  output throttles to what the planet can sustain.

Dreadnought replaces the former Deathstar name and artwork in the catalog. It
remains unavailable to build; this is a naming change, not another playable ship.

## Less work between planning and launching

Galaxy rows make debris, player status, alliance mates, and fleet actions easier
to scan. Quick harvest suggests a recycler count and lets you send directly from
the galaxy on desktop or mobile. Fleet slot counts stay close to those actions.

Dispatch combines coordinate entry, owned-planet selection, and destination
preview. You can maximize one cargo resource without replacing the others. Filling
all cargo preserves entered amounts and shares remaining capacity while reserving
fuel.

Combat simulations show surviving cargo capacity and estimated loot across the
sampled battles. Attack planning adds flight-time and fuel estimates, and
**Prepare attack** carries your selected force into Dispatch for launch review.
Report observations can be stale, and estimated loot is gross loot before fuel
costs. Preparing an attack doesn't launch it.

## Everyday fixes

Buildings have quick-build controls, and unaffordable catalog items have a clearer
visual treatment. Unbuilt building details focus on the first level. Manufacturing
shows resource costs and energy output more clearly, while active research appears
on its technology page.

Report rows are easier to open and distinguish by type. Harvest reports show the
resources collected on that mission separately from cargo already aboard. Sharing
a report stars it automatically; deleting it revokes its links permanently. Shared
links also have richer previews, limited to the information you chose to publish.

Mobile navigation, quantity editing, alliance notice editing, planet temperatures,
and location previews have received fixes. The public homepage includes more of
the game and loads its game interface separately.

## Upgrading a self-hosted universe

**Upgrading from 0.4.0 requires a coordinated shutdown. Do not mix old and new
processes across this migration.**

1. Stop every old API, worker, and operator process, and drain their database
   connections, including prepared connections. Take and verify a complete
   PostgreSQL backup, retaining the previous image and recording the backup cutoff.
2. Remove `ORBIT_LOCAL_INSECURE`, even if it is set to `0`. Set `ORBIT_ORIGIN` to
   the browser-facing HTTPS address; HTTPS uses Secure cookies. An intentionally
   HTTP installation must explicitly set `ORBIT_ALLOW_INSECURE_HTTP=1`.
3. Use the 0.5.0 binary with the service's database configuration to run
   `orbit db-init`. Migration 21 renames the universe policy table and adds bot
   participation; migration 22 adds messages, friendships, blocks, and circulars.
4. Start only 0.5.0 API and worker processes with the matching web assets. Verify
   readiness and export the universe policy before enabling any bots.
5. Preview and activate standard content with that same binary:

```sh
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id> --dry-run
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id>
orbit rules export --universe <id> --out rules-after.json
orbit audit --universe <id>
```

The upgrade command activates the entire bundled standard content, including
missing earlier additions. It preserves unrelated settings and reports conflicts
for customized or deliberately disabled content. Review those conflicts through
export/diff/apply; there is no force-overwrite option. If activation is pending,
keep workers running and repeat the same command with the same binary until
confirmed. Retain the effective revision, rules hash, and audit result, then
refresh clients.

Existing paid work retains its accepted costs and deadlines, and flights retain
committed fuel and travel terms. Combat uses the rules active at arrival. Deployment
alone does not activate new content or change an existing universe's speed.

Old binaries cannot restart against the migrated policy schema. Binary-only
rollback is unsupported: recovery requires the complete pre-upgrade backup with
its matching old binary, accounting for activity after the backup. Custom data or
clients using the old `Deathstar` enum need separate compatibility review; the new
wire name is `Dreadnought` and there is no legacy alias.

See the [migration and bot operations guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.5.0/docs/operations/bots.md#upgrade-and-compatibility)
and [rules upgrade guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.5.0/docs/operations/rules-updates.md).

## Starting and operating your own server

`orbit setup` now applies migrations and creates the first universe with the
bundled content. Repeating it preserves existing settings and progress; it doesn't
upgrade an existing world's content. PostgreSQL and credentials must already
exist. See the [setup guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.5.0/docs/operations/universe-configuration.md#fresh-installation).

Operators can change universe speed through the ordered rules workflow. Accrued
production settles at the old rate, while already-paid work and flights keep
their commitments. This release doesn't select a new speed for your universe.

Identified machine players are available through operator-managed enrollment and
bearer tokens. They use ordinary gameplay rules and starting resources, display a
Bot label, and have an enforced daily eight-hour sleep interval and an
operator-configured action allowance. Bots start disabled. This release supplies
the participation API, not a bot strategy or population manager.

Numbered container images are available through GHCR for Linux amd64 and arm64 at
`ghcr.io/unbought-games/orbit-imperium:0.5.0`. Publication includes a digest record
for immutable deployments and the downloadable amd64 image archive. There is no
floating `latest` tag. Obsolete Fly deployment files have been removed; follow the
current [deployment guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.5.0/docs/architecture/deployment.md).
