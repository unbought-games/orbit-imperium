You can now send Dreadnoughts to destroy an enemy moon. Getting there starts at home. A level 12 Research Lab and 300,000 energy let you research Graviton Technology, which unlocks the ship for yards that meet its other requirements.

The gamble comes after the battle. Even if your fleet wins, it can lose every ship on the mission. Will you take the risk?

## From Solar Satellites to a Dreadnought

Graviton counts all the energy your planet produces, including power already going to mines. Research takes one second and costs no materials. Once you earn the level, your whole empire keeps it even if you lose the satellites. When ordering Solar Satellites, you can now see how much energy the batch will add at your current production setting.

With Graviton 1, Hyperspace Technology 6, and Hyperspace Drive 7, any level 12 Shipyard on a planet or moon can build a Dreadnought. Each costs 5 million metal, 4 million crystal, and 1 million deuterium. It's slow, carries a million resources, and has rapid fire against targets ranging from fighters to battleships. You can try the matchup in the combat simulator before paying for the ship. [Dreadnought stats and rapid fire](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.8.0/docs/operations/dreadnought.md)

## What happens to the moon and the fleets

Choose **Destroy Moon** in Galaxy or Dispatch. You can send escorts. Your fleet fights the defenders first, and at least one Dreadnought must survive a victory to attempt destruction. More surviving Dreadnoughts improve the chance of destroying the moon. A larger moon is harder to destroy and more dangerous to your fleet. The two rolls are independent. Your fleet can destroy the moon and still lose every ship and all the loot on the way out.

A destroyed moon takes its buildings, local assets, and unfinished orders with it. Fleets already flying home to that moon return to its parent planet on schedule. Incoming transports, deployments, and allied holds also go to the planet. An enemy attack on its way to the moon reaches the empty coordinates and turns back. A later moon at those coordinates has its own identity. [Moon destruction and flight outcomes](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.8.0/docs/operations/moon-destruction.md)

## Planning fleets and reading reports

- **Load the resources available at departure.** Add all and Max reserve flight fuel before filling cargo. Typed amounts stay exact. If a refreshed quote changes any cargo amount by at least 10%, Fleet Review asks you to confirm it. Browser Back also restores a prepared fleet after a trip to another game screen in the same tab.
- **See what a raid can bring home.** The simulator shows average surviving units beside the inputs, potential loot, surviving cargo capacity, and average gross haul by resource. You pay launch fuel separately. The simulator remembers which sections you open, and you can review an attack from an imported spy report with the planning section closed.
- **Read a report from the commander orb.** Previews show the location, age, and outcome, including whether you attacked or defended and what your recyclers picked up. The inbox refreshes when you return to a tab. Old reports stay quiet when you archive or catch up, and manually recalling a fleet no longer creates an extra report.
- **Archive a shared report deliberately.** The detail page asks before archiving revokes a share link. Moving the report back to the inbox keeps the old link closed. You can archive a starred report and keep its star.

## Around your empire

Resources and Facilities show how many fields you’ve used on the selected planet or moon. Open the field count to see how many are reserved for construction. On phones, building rows show the next upgrade price. The Shipyard and defenses quantity fields can fill the largest order you can afford so you can check its cost before placing it.

You can rename your empire from its overview and see its emblem beside the name. Metal, crystal, and deuterium have consistent rows for balances on worlds and in flight. Emblems stay visible during profile refreshes, and each empire keeps its chosen emblem when you edit a shared template elsewhere.

Universe settings explains which materials from destroyed ships become debris under your universe's rules. Help & community has optional ways to support the game, and the account link opens a separate tab on desktop and mobile. If an order's response is interrupted, you can retry it in the current session. Reloading opens the server's current state and discards local retry attempts.

## Upgrading from 0.7.0

**Existing servers need an intermediate build before starting 0.8.0.** Migration 0051 drops the old transition journal. Back up PostgreSQL and persistent asset storage and retain a recovery point from before the bridge. Fresh installations can start directly with 0.8.0.

1. Build and deploy the journal-independent bridge from [commit 3c99912](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/commit/3c99912e035bce75fa746fd821a387a2aef2d383). Its migrations stop at 0050. Migrations 0048–0049 update appearance storage and 0050 adds committed activity capture. Pause cosmetic changes during the API transition and remove old APIs from identity-serving traffic before resuming them.
2. Upgrade every API, worker, maintenance writer, and standby that can reconnect. Disable restart paths for old writers. With the journal still present, verify orders, receipts, scheduled work, retries after restart, and delivery to reconnecting players. Confirm the journal receives no writes and check for database dependencies before proceeding. Once the bridge has processed work, a 0.7.0 binary is unsafe for rollback.
3. Run schema preparation with the 0.8.0 build. Migration 0051 removes the journal and refuses to drop dependent objects. Migration 0052 adds destroyed-moon records. Retain a compatible rollback build and the backup. Returning to journal-dependent software requires restoring compatible data. [Journal removal and rollback guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.8.0/docs/operations/journal-removal.md)

The standard rules bundle is **0.19.0**. Fresh universes receive it at setup. For an existing universe, deploy matching API, workers, web client, and operator CLI first. Preview the rules diff, resolve conflicts with custom rules, apply it, and record the resulting revision and audit. Keep readers for accepted missions and their reports if you later disable new launches. [Rules upgrade guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.8.0/docs/operations/rules-updates.md)

```sh
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id> --dry-run
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id>
orbit rules export --universe <id> --out rules-after.json
orbit audit --universe <id>
```

Operators can follow an order from admission to execution in structured logs and inspect request latency by route, action, and outcome. Set `ORBIT_LOG_LEVEL` and restart to change verbosity. The default is `info`. [Logging guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.8.0/docs/operations/logging.md) · [Request latency guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.8.0/docs/operations/request-latency.md)
