Orbit Imperium 0.4.0 adds alliances! You can recruit players, assign roles,
share a private noticeboard, and compare your alliance with the rest of the
universe.

There are also more things to build: Large Cargo ships, Solar Satellites, Heavy
Lasers, and Gauss Cannons. Alongside those additions, we've made reports, fleet
planning, and everyday navigation easier to use.

## Play together

Alliances can specify their recruitment type, whether open to anyone, accepting
applications, or closed. Custom roles let leaders delegate recruitment, member
management, and other responsibilities. Information can be made private and only
accessible to certain roles. And when you're done, leadership can be transferred
to someone else.

Alliances have public pages and a private board with pinned posts. Alliance tags
appear alongside player identities, and alliance rankings join the player high
scores. Daily rank movement shows how positions have changed once a prior daily
observation is available.

## More cargo, energy, and defenses

- **Large Cargo** carries up to 25,000 units. It requires Shipyard 4 and
  Combustion Drive 6.
- **Solar Satellites** produce energy based on the planet's maximum temperature.
  They require Shipyard 1, stay at their planet, and can be destroyed in combat.
  Resource settings show their output and let you adjust their production.
- **Heavy Lasers** require Shipyard 4, Energy Technology 3, and Laser Technology 6.
- **Gauss Cannons** require Shipyard 6, Energy Technology 6, Weapons Technology 3,
  and Shielding Technology 1.

Existing universes need explicit content activation after deployment. These
notes describe the numbered release; official-server deployment and activation
are verified separately.

## Reports you can actually use

Reports can be sorted, starred, and cleaned up in bulk. Titles and previews show
more useful player, planet, and resource information, while espionage research
is grouped by purpose.

You can open a combat simulation from an espionage report. It uses the report's
observed information, so hidden sections still need your assumptions. A report
is a snapshot, not a promise that the target still has the same defenses.

Combat and espionage reports can also be shared through revocable, unlisted
links. Names and target coordinates are hidden by default; you choose whether
to include them. Anyone with the link can read it, and revoking it can't recall
copies someone has already saved.

## Clearer planning and navigation

The catalog includes technical data for buildings, research, ships, and defenses.
Ship comparisons show base and researched statistics. Mine upgrades explain the
change to planet totals, and cost previews distinguish storage limits from a
shortage you can solve by waiting.

Fleet screens show stationed ships and carried resources. Dispatch review makes
the destination, cargo, fuel, and travel times easier to check before launch.
Incoming hostile fleets pulse the mobile header, and fleet countdowns retain
seconds when they matter.

Coordinates support direct actions and shareable links. Galaxy and system fields
accept typed destinations, the planet selector includes more context, and planet
renaming works inline in Empire. The Build menu is now **Develop**. Energy in the
header opens resource settings, where sliders adjust production percentages.

Construction and research cancellation asks for confirmation after 30 seconds
and explains the elapsed work you'll lose. Immediate undo stays quick. Orders
and dispatch forms clear quantities after success, and manufacturing affordability
tracks current resources more reliably.

## Upgrading a self-hosted universe

Back up your database and retain the previous image and active rules. Install
`orbit-imperium:0.4.0` across every API, worker, and operator process before
activating new content. Startup applies migrations 16–20 for daily ranking
observations, report stars and sharing, alliances, and report alliance identities.
There is no universe reset or inventory backfill.

Alliances, rankings, and report tools become available with the compatible
application deployment. The four new units use the existing rules workflow:
export the intended universe's current configuration, prepare a proposal, review
`orbit rules diff`, then apply with the deployed binary. Use these scripts from
the release source:

- `scripts/prepare-heavy-laser-rules.mjs`
- `scripts/prepare-gauss-cannon-rules.mjs`
- `scripts/prepare-large-cargo-rules.mjs`
- `scripts/prepare-solar-satellite-rules.mjs`

Each takes an exported input file and a fresh output path. Start each activation
from a fresh export, preserving universe identity, base revision, speed, topology,
and unrelated custom settings. Heavy Laser needs enabled Laser Technology; the
other prerequisites must also have complete enabled definitions. The bundled
complete rules files are test fixtures, not replacements for a live export.
Activating Large Cargo before Solar Satellites includes their rapid-fire pairing;
the scripts also handle the reverse order.

Export again after each confirmed activation and run `orbit audit` after the
rollout. A pending scoped activation remains queued; retry the identical proposal
to observe its result. Existing paid jobs retain payment and timing, and flights
retain their committed travel and fuel terms. Combat uses the rules active at
arrival. Refresh clients after deployment and activation.

To stop new construction, disable the relevant catalog entry in a forward rules
update. Keep the definitions needed by existing units and paid work. In particular,
retain satellite energy and combat definitions; an older binary that cannot read
them is not a safe rollback after activation.
