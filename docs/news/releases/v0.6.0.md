Orbit Imperium 0.6.0 lets you attack together and defend each other's planets.
Invite other players into a coordinated attack, send fleets to hold at a friend's
planet, and keep visiting defenders supplied through the Alliance Depot.

There's more to build, too. Battleships, Battlecruisers, and Destroyers join the
fleet, while Nanite Factories speed up industry and Terraformers make room on
crowded planets. We've also reworked navigation, reports, and dispatch so it's
easier to keep track of the empire you're building.

These notes describe the numbered open-source release. Availability on an existing
server depends on its deployment and content activation.

## Attack and defend together

An attack group can coordinate up to five players and sixteen fleets against one
target. Launch an attack, create a group from its fleet card, and invite players.
They can join from Fleets or the command HUD. Alliance membership isn't required;
every participant uses their own ships, fleet slots, and fuel. Joining fleets can
delay the shared arrival within the game's timing limits, and each sender can
recall their own fleets.

All attackers fight in one battle with their own combat research. Surviving fleets
share the available loot according to their free cargo capacity and return
independently. The simulator now supports separate allied fleets and research
assumptions on both sides. It retains its 2,000 combined-unit limit.

You can also send a Hold mission to a friend's or alliance mate's planet. Visiting
fleets defend alongside its owner until their paid holding time expires or their
sender recalls them. The host's Alliance Depot can spend local deuterium to extend
their stay. Holding uses the sender's ordinary fleet slot; it doesn't grant access
to the host's private resources or other movements.

See the [attack group guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.6.0/docs/operations/alliance-attacks.md)
and [holding and Depot guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.6.0/docs/operations/alliance-depot.md)
for timing, capacity, and resupply rules.

## More ways to develop your empire

- **Hyperspace Technology and Hyperspace Drive** unlock Battleships,
  Battlecruisers, and Destroyers. Bombers switch to Hyperspace propulsion at
  completed Drive level 8. Already-launched fleets keep their accepted travel
  terms. Hyperspace Technology itself gives no per-level cargo or speed bonus.
- **Nanite Factories** accelerate construction, demolition, and manufacturing.
  Each completed level halves the raw duration before rounding and the existing
  construction adjustment. Research is unaffected. Nanite work cannot overlap
  unfinished local manufacturing.
- **Terraformers** add five fields per level and occupy one themselves, leaving
  four more free fields. They require Nanite Factory 1, Energy Technology 12,
  enough gross energy when construction starts, and one free field. They cannot
  be demolished.
- **Missile Silos** store Anti-Ballistic and Interplanetary Missiles. Offensive
  missiles can destroy defenses without a fleet battle; interceptors stop them
  one for one. Missile losses don't produce debris or defense repairs, and a
  launched missile cannot be recalled.
- **Colony abandonment** lets you remove a colony after reviewing the assets and
  unfinished work you'll lose. Your homeworld and last planet are protected,
  and dependent fleets, allied holds, or active research can block removal. The
  coordinates become available again at the second UTC midnight after abandonment.

Operators can opt into deuterium in debris through universe rules. It is off by
default, uses the existing ship debris percentage, and includes destroyed ships'
construction deuterium rather than fuel or cargo. Turning it off later preserves
existing debris. Moon gameplay and Dreadnought construction remain unavailable.

## Less friction in everyday play

The command HUD brings reports, fleets, and incoming attacks together on mobile
and desktop. Desktop navigation has a persistent sidebar, Galaxy and Fleets have
separate destinations, and the combat simulator can open directly from navigation.
The command orb reserves red for active attacks.

Dispatch has clearer mission choices, available-ship lists, cargo capacity, and a
compact review. Galaxy debris fields open quick harvest. Empire totals include
resources aboard fleets, and production queues show quantities and completion
more clearly. Building forecasts remain available during construction and at full
storage.

Reports share their state between the full screen and the HUD. You can archive
reports without revoking shared links, select multiple reports more easily, and
keep using controls while background refreshes run. Combat reports retain
historical participant locations. Archiving returns you to the list after the
server confirms it.

Private messages now read as chat conversations. Player dossiers bring public
identity and scores together, and an optional empire insignia gives that identity
a visual mark. The community release includes free symbols and the editor;
cosmetics provide no gameplay advantage.

## Upgrading a self-hosted universe

Back up PostgreSQL and retain the previous image before upgrading from 0.5.0.
Apply migrations **0023–0029** using the 0.6.0 binary and your service's database
configuration (`orbit db-init`). These add world-edit receipts, missile flights,
colony abandonment, insignias, public feeds, allied holding, and attack groups.

Deploy compatible API, worker, operator binaries, and matching web assets across
all instances before activating new content or using online world edits. Old
processes don't participate in the world-edit transaction gate or understand the
new holding and attack-group commitments.

Insignias introduce persistent asset storage. For a single-host container, mount
`/data/assets`, make it writable by UID 10001, and back it up with the database.
Multiple hosts need shared S3-compatible storage. API startup verifies asset
publication and refuses readiness if storage fails. The default cosmetic policy
unlocks installed content without an external account service. Additional catalogs
are installed separately; community images don't contain official supporter-only
originals. See the [insignia operations guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.6.0/docs/operations/empire-customization.md).

Existing universes retain their saved rules until explicitly upgraded. Preview
and activate the bundled content with the same 0.6.0 binary:

```sh
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id> --dry-run
orbit rules upgrade --universe <id>
orbit rules export --universe <id> --out rules-after.json
orbit audit --universe <id>
```

The standard content bundle is **0.15.0**, versioned independently of this
application release. Fresh setup includes it; upgrading installs missing earlier
additions too. Customized definitions or deliberately disabled content produce
conflicts for review. Already-paid work and fleets retain their accepted terms;
combat uses the rules active at contact. Deployment alone changes neither the
selected content nor universe speed.

If activation is pending, keep workers running and repeat the same command with
the same binary until confirmed. Record the effective rules revision/hash and
audit result, then refresh clients. Do not restore an old binary after new formats
or commitments have been written unless it understands them. Turning a feature
off doesn't reverse its data format. Recovery to an incompatible older release
requires a matching pre-upgrade backup, accounting for activity since that backup.
See the [rules upgrade guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.6.0/docs/operations/rules-updates.md).

## Tools for operators and community projects

The new `orbit world` commands inspect and edit worlds, create local scenarios,
and manage playgrounds. They require database access and bypass ordinary payment
and waiting. Deploy matching serving processes before using online edits. Scenario
exports refuse active missiles, allied holds, attack groups, and unexpired Depot
supply budgets rather than dropping commitments; database backups retain them.
The old binary `dev-seed` and `dev-reset` commands are retired. See
[world management](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.6.0/docs/operations/world-management.md).

Anonymous public JSON feeds expose player rankings hourly and occupied planets
daily at UTC midnight. Everyone receives the same scheduled observation; faster
polling doesn't reveal fresher data. Feeds exclude private resources, research,
fleets, and espionage. Keep a worker running to publish them. A fresh or reset
universe returns `503` until its first scheduled publication, so the planet feed
can wait until the next UTC midnight. See the
[public API guide](https://github.com/unbought-games/orbit-imperium/blob/v0.6.0/docs/operations/public-universe-api.md).

Numbered images are published at `ghcr.io/unbought-games/orbit-imperium:0.6.0` for
Linux amd64 and arm64, with a digest record and downloadable amd64 image archive.
There is no floating `latest` tag.
